3 Key Takeaways:

  • One of the biggest takeaways from building the no-code toolkit was learning how to create multi-step interactions in Unity that are accessible and editable for ENG students without programming experience. I wanted each of the four components, along with the grab interactable, to provide clear and simple ways to build interactions without requiring them to fully understand the technical side of Unity.
  • The built-in grab component was especially helpful because it provided a straightforward way to pick up and interact with objects in a 3D environment. I tried to replicate that simplicity and hands-on experience in my own toolkit.

How this connects to Southwood?

AI think this toolkit will allow our English teammates to take a much more active role in developing the SoXR experience. Rather than relying entirely on the technical team to create every interaction, ENG students can help develop the narration and sequencing of events that tell the stories of our co-creator. Although, the toolkit has its limitations and components can only accomplish so much, and there may still be smaller technical details or more complicated interactions that require CS expertise, I think the toolkit provides a strong foundation for English students to independently experiment with and build interactions.


Challenges I Faced

The most challenging part of this assignment was explaining to my English teammates, in non-technical language, how to use the toolkit within our project. I often found myself using technical terms that they were unfamiliar with, which made it difficult to communicate exactly what each component did in the toolkit. i had to think outside the box and find different ways to explain the purpose of each tool and how it could be applied to our specific project.
